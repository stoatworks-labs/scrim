Photosensitivity note

Scrim adds no flashes of its own: nothing in it moves with time. But its lights brighten both clips, about four times at the defaults, so a clip's own flicker can become a large flash, and stepping a light fast (Front Light, Back Light, Wash or Opacity, or a preset after a preset) flashes the whole picture. And with Draw Mesh on, the weave beats against the pixels in a moiré, a regular pattern of fine stripes; Draw Mesh off removes it. Flashing light and striped patterns can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy.

At its defaults it goes over the line on 2 of 33 pairs of Resolume's demo clips: AV/Beat 002 projected over Shop74/BattleWeapon_Tank_09, and AV/Synth 006 over Shop74/NeonRoom2_32. In those pairs' worst second, over a third of the picture, the brightness swings 6 and 4 times on its own and 8 times through the plugin, which is four flashes a second (more than three is over the line). The view this page opens on, the Geometry card projected over the Synthetic scene at the defaults, was measured the same way for 40 seconds at 60 frames a second: no swing over the whole picture, and one in its worst third; each preset's view, 12 seconds each, one at most. A clip of your own is brightened the same way. Pause, under the picture, stops it at once.
